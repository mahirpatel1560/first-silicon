TITLE
Feedback wanted: a free daily list of hardware internships labeled by discipline, class year and ITAR. 172 of 191 postings don't say what year you need to be

BODY
I'm an electrical engineering student at the University of Illinois. I built First Silicon, a free list of every hardware internship and co-op on the company job boards it reads, updated every morning: https://firstsilicon.pages.dev/?ref=r-ee

As of September 24 it has 191 roles at 34 companies (the numbers change daily). Each role is tagged by discipline (test, PCB, controls, embedded, analog, FPGA/ASIC, RF, power, photonics, semiconductor process), by class year, and with flags for US citizenship, ITAR/export control and clearance.

The class-year split: 4 postings say freshmen or sophomores can apply, 15 ask for juniors or later, and 172 don't say anything about class year.

There are also short guides for each discipline: what interns actually do, the skills to show, and a few starter projects (https://firstsilicon.pages.dev/disciplines/).

Three questions for the engineers here:
1. When a posting doesn't state a class year, would your team look at a strong sophomore? The site's advice for those 172 is: apply unless the posting says juniors/seniors or a graduation date you can't meet. Tell me if that's wrong for your field.
2. If you've hosted interns, what would you add to the discipline guides? What made a first- or second-year applicant stand out?
3. Which hardware companies should be on the list? It only reads Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby boards today, so companies on Workday and similar systems are missing.

Labels are automatic and can be wrong; every role links to the employer's own posting.
